Receiving reviewer comments on your research paper can be stressful. After spending months conducting research, analyzing data, and writing your manuscript, you may receive several pages of criticism and suggestions for improvement.

Some reviewers may question your methodology. Others may ask for additional experiments, stronger arguments, or more recent references.

But here is something every researcher should understand: Receiving reviewer comments is a normal part of academic publishing.

Even experienced researchers receive major revision requests. The important thing is not how many comments you receive, but how effectively you address them.

A thoughtful response can improve your manuscript, demonstrate your scientific reasoning, and increase your chances of publication.

In this article, we discuss 10 practical tips for responding to journal reviewer comments, with real-world examples to help PhD students and researchers navigate the peer-review process.

1. Read All Reviewer Comments Carefully
One of the biggest mistakes researchers make is responding to comments without fully understanding them.

Before making any changes, read the editor's decision letter and all reviewer comments carefully.

Identify whether the reviewers are concerned about your methodology, novelty, results, literature review, or presentation.

For example, a reviewer might write:

"The contribution of this study is not sufficiently clear."

This does not necessarily mean your research lacks novelty. It may simply mean you have not explained your contribution effectively.

What should you do?

Read the comment in the context of your entire paper. Identify where the issue occurs and determine what needs clarification.

Also, pay attention to the editor's instructions because some concerns may be more important than others.

2. Stay Professional and Avoid Emotional Responses
It can be frustrating when reviewers criticize work you have spent months or years developing.

However, responding defensively can negatively affect the revision process.

For example:

Reviewer comment:

"The methodology is not sufficiently justified."

Poor response:

"The reviewer has misunderstood our methodology."

Better response:

"We thank the reviewer for highlighting this concern. We have expanded Section 3 to explain the rationale behind our methodological choices and clarify why the selected approach is appropriate for our research objectives."

The second response is respectful and focuses on improving the manuscript.

Remember that reviewers evaluate your research, not your personal abilities.

A professional response is always more effective than an emotional argument.

3. Address Every Reviewer Comment Individually
Ignoring reviewer comments is one of the most common mistakes during manuscript revision.

Even if a comment seems minor or repetitive, it deserves consideration.

A good approach is to respond to each comment separately and explain the action taken.

Reviewer comment:

"The authors should explain the dataset selection criteria."

Suggested response:

"Thank you for this suggestion. We have revised Section 3.1 to provide a detailed explanation of the dataset selection criteria, including data availability, relevance to the research objectives, and suitability for the proposed experiments."

This approach makes it easier for reviewers to understand how their feedback was addressed.

For multiple reviewers, organize comments systematically so that none are overlooked.

Practical tip: Number the reviewer comments and track the corresponding manuscript changes.

 
TIP 04 OF 10

4. Clearly Explain the Changes Made to Your Manuscript
Simply saying "The manuscript has been revised" is not enough.

Reviewers need to understand what you changed and where they can find those changes.

Reviewer comment:

"The discussion section does not adequately explain the limitations of the proposed method."

Weak response:

"We have improved the discussion."

Better response:

"We appreciate this comment. We have expanded Section 5 to discuss three limitations of the proposed approach: dataset size, computational complexity, and limited external validation. We have also explained how these limitations may affect the generalizability of our findings."

The better response provides specific information about the revision.

Whenever possible, mention the relevant section, page, or line numbers.

This saves reviewers time and makes your response more convincing.

 
TIP 05 OF 10

5. Support Your Responses With Scientific Evidence
Reviewers may challenge your findings, assumptions, or methodological decisions.

In such cases, simply stating that your approach is correct is not sufficient.

You should support your explanation with relevant evidence, such as experimental results, established research methods, or published studies.

Reviewer comment:

"The selected machine learning algorithm may not be appropriate for this classification problem."

Suggested response:

"We appreciate the reviewer's concern. We selected the algorithm because it is suitable for structured datasets and provides interpretable predictions. We have added supporting references in Section 3 and expanded the discussion of our model selection criteria."

When appropriate, additional experiments or statistical analyses may strengthen your response.

However, never invent results or cite studies that do not support your argument.

Scientific evidence is more persuasive than personal opinion.

 
TIP 06 OF 10

6. Know How to Politely Disagree With Reviewers
You do not have to agree with every reviewer suggestion.

Sometimes reviewers recommend changes that are outside the scope of your research or are not methodologically appropriate.

In such situations, you can respectfully disagree while providing a clear justification.

Reviewer comment:

"The authors should replace the proposed machine learning approach with a deep learning model."

Suggested response:

"We thank the reviewer for this suggestion. While deep learning models are effective in many applications, the primary objective of this study is to investigate interpretable machine learning techniques using a relatively small structured dataset. We believe the selected approach is more appropriate for the current research objectives. We have clarified this decision in the revised methodology section."

This response acknowledges the suggestion without unnecessarily changing the research direction.

The key is to explain your decision logically and respectfully.

You can disagree with a reviewer, but your disagreement should be supported by scientific reasoning.

 
TIP 07 OF 10

7. Handle Requests for Additional Experiments Carefully
Reviewers often request additional experiments, comparisons, or validation.

For example, a reviewer may ask:

"The authors should compare their proposed approach with more baseline methods."

If the request is reasonable and feasible, conducting additional experiments can strengthen your manuscript.

Suggested response:

"Thank you for this valuable suggestion. We have conducted additional experiments using two baseline methods. The results are presented in Table 4, and the comparison has been discussed in Section 4.2."

However, some requests may not be feasible because of data limitations, ethical restrictions, or the scope of the research.

In such cases, explain the limitations clearly.

For example:

"We agree that additional external validation would strengthen the findings. However, a suitable independent dataset is not currently available. We have acknowledged this limitation in the revised manuscript and identified external validation as an important direction for future research."

Avoid dismissing requests simply because they require additional work.

Instead, explain what is scientifically possible and how you have addressed the underlying concern.

 
TIP 08 OF 10

8. Strengthen Your Research Novelty and Contributions
One of the most challenging reviewer comments is:

"The novelty of this research is unclear."

This comment can be discouraging, particularly when you believe your study makes an important contribution.

However, the problem may be how you communicate your contribution rather than the contribution itself.

How should you respond?

Review your introduction and related work sections.

Explain how your research differs from existing studies and what new knowledge it provides.

For example:

"We appreciate the reviewer's observation. We have revised the Introduction to clarify the main contributions of our study. Specifically, we now highlight the differences between our approach and existing methods, including the evaluation strategy and application context."

You may also include a comparison table summarizing previous studies and their limitations.

However, avoid exaggerated novelty claims.

Statements such as "This is the first study ever conducted on this topic" should only be used when supported by a thorough examination of the literature.

 
TIP 09 OF 10

9. Make Sure Your Manuscript Matches Your Responses
A common mistake is explaining changes in the response document without making those changes in the manuscript.

For example, you may tell a reviewer that you have expanded the discussion of limitations, but the revised paper still contains the original paragraph.

This can create doubts about the quality of your revision.

Before resubmitting, verify that every claimed change has been implemented.

Check whether:

Revised explanations appear in the correct sections.
Additional experiments are accurately reported.
Figures and tables match the updated results.
New references are included in the reference list.
Section and page numbers in your responses are correct.
Changes in one section are consistent with the rest of the paper.
If the journal requests tracked changes or highlighted revisions, follow those instructions carefully.

A clear connection between your responses and manuscript revisions makes the review process easier for everyone involved.

 
TIP 10 OF 10

10. Review Everything Before Resubmitting
After addressing all reviewer comments, do not rush to resubmit your manuscript.

Take time to review the entire submission carefully.

Check your revised paper for grammatical errors, inconsistent terminology, missing references, and formatting problems.

Also, review your responses to ensure they are professional, complete, and supported by the revised manuscript.

If possible, ask a co-author to review the responses before submission.

A fresh perspective can help identify unclear explanations or overlooked comments.

Finally, confirm that you have followed the journal's resubmission instructions and met the revision deadline.

Remember: A carefully prepared revision demonstrates that you have taken the peer-review process seriously.

Can AI Help You Respond to Journal Reviewer Comments?
AI tools such as ChatGPT can be useful during the manuscript revision process.

They can help researchers organize reviewer comments, improve the clarity of responses, and identify language that sounds defensive or unclear.

For example, you can ask an AI tool to suggest a more professional way to express a disagreement with a reviewer.

AI can also help you check whether your response clearly addresses the concern raised.

However, AI should not be used to generate fake experimental results, invent references, or claim that manuscript changes have been completed when they have not.

Always verify AI-generated suggestions and follow the journal's confidentiality and AI-use policies before sharing unpublished manuscripts or reviewer correspondence with external tools.

Use AI to improve your communication, not to replace your scientific judgment.

Final Thoughts
Responding to journal reviewer comments is an important skill that every researcher should develop.

Whether you are a PhD student submitting your first research paper or an experienced academic, critical feedback is a normal part of scientific publishing.

The best approach is to remain professional, address comments systematically, support your decisions with evidence, and make meaningful improvements to your manuscript.

Remember that reviewers are not always correct, and you do not need to accept every suggestion. However, you should carefully consider every concern and explain your decisions clearly.

A successful response to reviewers is not about defending every sentence of your original manuscript. It is about demonstrating that your research is rigorous, your decisions are justified, and your paper has improved.

With patience and a structured approach, even challenging reviewer comments can become opportunities to strengthen your research and improve your chances of publication.
